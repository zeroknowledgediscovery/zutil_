\title{Entropy-Rate Bounds for Stochastic Codewords\\\large Typical-Set Packing and the Binary Symmetric Channel Example}
\author{Technical Note}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{abstract}
A stochastic codeword is a probability law over length-$L$ output strings rather than a single deterministic string.  If each codeword law is generated by a stationary ergodic binary source of entropy rate $h$, the asymptotic equipartition property (AEP) says that essentially all of its probability is carried by about $2^{Lh}$ strings.  Since the ambient binary space contains $2^L$ strings, a simple packing argument suggests at most $2^{L(1-h)}$ reliably distinguishable stochastic codewords, or rate $1-h$ bits per emitted symbol.  This note states a precise version of that converse under a uniform AEP condition, separates the converse from the additional assumptions needed for achievability, and gives the binary symmetric channel (BSC) as a classical construction in which the corresponding stochastic-cloud rate $1-H_2(p)$ is actually achievable.  The BSC example also shows why low entropy is essential for rates close to one.  Finally, we state the open PFSA question: whether a sufficiently large family of low-entropy dynamical sources can attain the same packing exponent while remaining identifiable from finite realizations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Stochastic codewords}
Let $\X=\{0,1\}$.  For block length $L$, a stochastic codebook contains $M_L$ probability laws
\[
P_{m,L}\quad (m=1,\ldots,M_L)
\]
on $\X^L$.  To transmit message $m$, the encoder draws
\[
X^L\sim P_{m,L}.
\]
A decoder $\phi_L:\X^L\to\{1,\ldots,M_L\}$ estimates the message.  The maximal error probability is
\[
\Pe^{(L)}=\max_m P_{m,L}\{\phi_L(X^L)\neq m\}.
\]
The code rate is
\[
R_L=\frac{1}{L}\log_2 M_L\qquad\text{bits per emitted binary symbol}.
\]

The case of interest is that each $P_{m,L}$ is the length-$L$ marginal of a stationary ergodic stochastic source $G_m$ with entropy rate $h(G_m)=h$.  A message is therefore represented by the \emph{identity of a stochastic generator}; the actual observed string is one random realization from that generator.

\section{AEP volume of one stochastic codeword}
For a fixed stationary ergodic source $G$ with entropy rate $h$, the Shannon--McMillan--Breiman theorem gives
\[
-\frac1L\log_2 P_G(X^L)\longrightarrow h
\]
almost surely.  Equivalently, for every $\epsilon>0$, there are typical sets $\T_{G,L}^{(\epsilon)}$ such that
\[
P_G(\T_{G,L}^{(\epsilon)})\to1
\]
and every $x^L\in\T_{G,L}^{(\epsilon)}$ satisfies
\[
2^{-L(h+\epsilon)}\le P_G(x^L)\le 2^{-L(h-\epsilon)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
2^{L(h-\epsilon)}(1-o(1))
\le |\T_{G,L}^{(\epsilon)}|
\le 2^{L(h+\epsilon)}.
\]
Thus, at exponential scale, one entropy-rate-$h$ stochastic codeword occupies $2^{Lh}$ ordinary binary strings.

This is the origin of the heuristic
\[
\frac{2^L}{2^{Lh}}=2^{L(1-h)}
\]
for the number of stochastic codeword clouds that might fit in the ambient sequence space.  To make this a converse for a growing family of sources, however, one needs a uniform form of the AEP.

\section{A precise rate converse}
\begin{definition}[Uniform AEP family]
A sequence of stochastic codebooks $\{P_{m,L}:1\le m\le M_L\}$ has entropy exponent $h$ uniformly if for every $\epsilon>0$ there exists $L_0$ such that, for all $L\ge L_0$ and every $m\le M_L$, there is a set $\T_{m,L}$ satisfying
\[
P_{m,L}(\T_{m,L})\ge1-\epsilon
\]
and
\[
P_{m,L}(x^L)\le 2^{-L(h-\epsilon)}
\quad\text{for every }x^L\in\T_{m,L}.
\]
For a single fixed stationary ergodic source this follows from AEP; for a codebook whose number of distinct sources grows exponentially with $L$, uniformity is an additional condition and must not be silently assumed.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Entropy-rate packing converse]
Consider binary stochastic codebooks satisfying the uniform AEP condition with entropy exponent $h$.  If the maximal decoding error obeys $\Pe^{(L)}\to0$, then
\[
\limsup_{L\to\infty}\frac1L\log_2 M_L\le 1-h.
\]
Equivalently, every reliably decodable rate satisfies
\[
\boxed{R\le1-h.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
D_{m,L}=\{x^L:\phi_L(x^L)=m\}
\]
be the decoder region for message $m$.  The $D_{m,L}$ are pairwise disjoint and, by maximal reliability,
\[
P_{m,L}(D_{m,L})\ge1-\Pe^{(L)}.
\]
Fix $\epsilon>0$ and choose the uniform typical set $\T_{m,L}$.  Then
\[
P_{m,L}(D_{m,L}\cap\T_{m,L})
\ge 1-\Pe^{(L)}-\epsilon.
\]
Every point of $\T_{m,L}$ has probability at most $2^{-L(h-\epsilon)}$.  Therefore
\[
1-\Pe^{(L)}-\epsilon
\le |D_{m,L}\cap\T_{m,L}|\,2^{-L(h-\epsilon)},
\]
so
\[
|D_{m,L}|
\ge (1-\Pe^{(L)}-\epsilon)2^{L(h-\epsilon)}.
\]
Summing over messages and using disjointness,
\begin{align*}
2^L
&\ge \sum_{m=1}^{M_L}|D_{m,L}|\\
&\ge M_L(1-\Pe^{(L)}-\epsilon)2^{L(h-\epsilon)}.
\end{align*}
Hence
\[
\frac1L\log_2M_L
\le 1-h+\epsilon
-\frac1L\log_2(1-\Pe^{(L)}-\epsilon).
\]
Take $L\to\infty$, then $\epsilon\downarrow0$, to obtain
\[
\limsup_{L\to\infty}R_L\le1-h.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does and does not prove]
The theorem is a converse.  AEP determines the exponential volume required by each stochastic codeword, but it does not itself prove that the ambient space can be tiled or packed by $2^{L(1-h)}$ distinguishable stationary ergodic sources.  Equality $R=1-h$ requires an achievability construction with sufficiently separated probability laws.
\end{remark}

\section{Conditional achievability statement}
The exact missing ingredient can be stated cleanly.

\begin{proposition}[Separated-family achievability]
Suppose that for every $R<1-h$ and all sufficiently large $L$ there exist
\[
M_L=\lfloor2^{LR}\rfloor
\]
stochastic laws $P_{m,L}$ and pairwise disjoint sets $D_{m,L}\subseteq\X^L$ such that
\[
\inf_{m\le M_L}P_{m,L}(D_{m,L})\longrightarrow1.
\]
Then every $R<1-h$ is achievable with vanishing maximal error.  Combined with the preceding converse, the stochastic-code capacity of that admissible family is $1-h$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the decoder $\phi_L(x^L)=m$ when $x^L\in D_{m,L}$, with an arbitrary rule outside their union.  Since the $D_{m,L}$ are disjoint,
\[
\Pe^{(L)}\le 1-\inf_mP_{m,L}(D_{m,L})\to0.
\]
Thus each $R<1-h$ is achievable.  The converse prevents any $R>1-h$ under the same uniform entropy condition.
\end{proof}

The proposition deliberately isolates the hard part: constructing exponentially many \emph{different stochastic generators} whose high-probability regions remain distinguishable.

\section{Why low entropy gives high rate}
The binary ambient space has exponent $1$ because $|\X^L|=2^L$.  A source with entropy rate $h$ consumes exponent $h$.  The remaining exponent available for source identity is therefore at most
\[
1-h.
\]
Thus low stochastic entropy is not incidental; it is the mechanism by which the identity of the generating process can carry a high rate.

At the extremes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item If $h\to0$, one stochastic codeword occupies only $2^{o(L)}$ strings, so the counting bound approaches one bit per emitted binary symbol.
\item If $h\to1$, one source occupies essentially all $2^L$ binary strings at exponential scale, leaving zero exponential room for source identity.
\end{itemize}

For an alphabet of size $q$, the identical argument gives
\[
R\le \log_2q-h,
\]
and the normalized rate is at most
\[
1-\frac{h}{\log_2q}.
\]

\section{The binary symmetric channel as an achievable stochastic-cloud example}
The classical BSC gives a concrete example where the counting exponent is not merely an upper bound but the exact achievable rate.

Choose a deterministic binary center $c_m\in\{0,1\}^L$ for message $m$ and transmit
\[
X^L=c_m\oplus Z^L,
\qquad
Z_i\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p),
\quad 0<p<\tfrac12.
\]
Conditioned on $c_m$, the transmitted string is therefore a stochastic cloud around $c_m$.  The noise entropy per symbol is
\[
h=\Hb(p)
=-p\log_2p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p).
\]
With high probability, $Z^L$ has approximately $pL$ ones.  The number of such typical noise patterns is
\[
\binom{L}{pL}\doteq 2^{L\Hb(p)},
\]
where $a_L\doteq2^{L\alpha}$ denotes equality at exponential order.  Consequently the typical received cloud around one codeword has volume exponent $\Hb(p)$, while the whole binary space has exponent $1$.  The packing heuristic gives
\[
M_L\doteq2^{L[1-\Hb(p)]}.
\]

For the BSC, Shannon's channel coding theorem proves achievability: for every
\[
R<1-\Hb(p)
\]
there exist length-$L$ codebooks with $M_L\approx2^{LR}$ and decoding error tending to zero.  Conversely, no larger rate is reliable.  Hence
\[
\boxed{C_{\mathrm{BSC}(p)}=1-\Hb(p).}
\]

This is exactly the same exponent obtained by ``ambient volume minus stochastic-cloud volume.''  The BSC therefore provides a rigorous existence example of high-rate stochastic-cloud coding.

\subsection{Numerical examples}
For $p=0.01$,
\[
\Hb(0.01)\approx0.080793,
\qquad
C\approx0.919207\ \text{bits/symbol}.
\]
Thus a stochastic cloud containing approximately $2^{0.0808L}$ typical realizations per deterministic center still permits roughly $2^{0.9192L}$ reliably distinguishable messages.

For $p=0.001$,
\[
\Hb(0.001)\approx0.011408,
\qquad
C\approx0.988592\ \text{bits/symbol}.
\]
Only one tenth of one percent crossover probability already gives a genuinely stochastic transmitted sequence while allowing a rate within about $1.14\%$ of the binary maximum.

\section{Relation to PFSA stochastic coding}
The BSC example should be interpreted carefully.  Its message-dependent law is a noisy cloud centered on a deterministic length-$L$ codeword.  It proves that the typical-set packing exponent $1-h$ can be achievable for stochastic codeword clouds.  It does \emph{not} by itself prove that an exponentially large family of stationary finite-state stochastic processes can attain the same exponent.

For PFSA coding, the desired construction is different.  A message $m$ selects a PFSA $G_m$, and the observed codeword is a length-$L$ realization
\[
X^L\sim G_m.
\]
The decoder must infer which dynamical law generated the realization.  If all machines have entropy rate approximately $h$, the converse above suggests the same fundamental ceiling $1-h$, but achievability requires enough process diversity and enough statistical separation.

A precise target theorem would therefore have the following form.

\begin{quote}
For every sufficiently small $h$ and every $R<1-h$, construct a family of $M_L\ge2^{LR}$ finite-stationary ergodic processes of entropy rate $h+o(1)$ such that the generating process can be identified from a length-$L$ realization with maximal error probability tending to zero.
\end{quote}

Proving this would establish
\[
C_{\mathrm{PFSA}}(h)=1-h
\]
for the specified admissible PFSA class.  Without such a construction, $1-h$ should be called the entropy-volume converse or packing limit, not yet the PFSA capacity.

\section{Connection to delimiter-free process coding}
The recent delimiter-free experiment illustrates a complementary issue.  There, information was represented by latent process identity and residence duration.  A decoder recovered process changes from higher-order temporal structure without being supplied explicit delimiters.  That experiment demonstrates finite-sample \emph{identifiability} of stochastic regimes; the theorem here concerns the asymptotic \emph{number} of distinct stochastic codewords that can coexist at a given entropy rate.

These are the two ingredients needed for a full stochastic coding theory:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Packing:} how many stochastic generators can be placed in sequence space at entropy rate $h$?
\item \textbf{Identification:} how long a realization is required to determine the active generator with a specified error probability?
\end{enumerate}
The AEP gives the natural packing exponent $1-h$; process divergence and finite-sample discrimination determine whether that exponent can actually be attained by a structured family such as PFSAs.

\section{Summary}
For binary stochastic codewords with a uniform entropy exponent $h$, reliable decoding implies
\[
R\le1-h.
\]
The proof is a direct consequence of AEP volume and disjoint decoding regions.  Equality is not automatic: it requires a family whose high-probability regions can be packed and distinguished at the corresponding exponential rate.  The BSC supplies the canonical achievable example,
\[
C=1-H_2(p),
\]
showing explicitly that low stochastic entropy permits rates arbitrarily close to one.  The central PFSA problem is therefore an achievability problem: determine whether low-entropy dynamical generators can be constructed in exponentially large, reliably distinguishable families approaching the same $1-h$ exponent.
